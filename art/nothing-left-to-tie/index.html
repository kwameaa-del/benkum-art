Nothing Left to Tie

I’ve checked the same knot three times now.

The knot’s already tight. I check it again anyway.

On September 1, 2026, Mathews Mulenga, head of Restoration for Community Justice, warned that Zambia’s record 5-million-metric-ton maize harvest this year could be undone within months — “This is not a Kanchibiya problem or a Muchinga problem,” he said. “This is a national emergency in the making.” Forecasters expect a punishing El Niño to peak by December, pushing the October 2026–March 2027 rainy season well below normal, echoing a 2023–24 drought that erased a similarly strong harvest almost as fast as it arrived. More than two million smallholder farmers are being urged to hold back part of this year’s grain instead of selling it, and to plant hardier, faster crops before the rains — if they come at all — fall short again.

I’ve checked the same knot three times now. My hands keep finding reasons to stay busy with the one thing they can still fix.
